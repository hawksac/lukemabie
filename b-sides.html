The Basement Tapes

Ars Nova · The Interstate Marathon

Before I ever worked in a real studio, there was my basement. Ars Nova and The Interstate Marathon were made down there with no studio at all, just me trying to figure out how to make things. I was really inspired by The Postal Service.

Back then it wasn't easy to get a MIDI keyboard talking to recording software, or to get it to trigger the sounds you wanted. I remember working out exactly how every MIDI channel connected to my keyboard, and which ones came first, just to get a song to play back right.

Looking back, that's where I learned both the creative side and the technical side, and it's probably what got me into tech. Go Motion and Anniversaire, further down, are where I learned to work in a studio.

B2

Go Motion

2006–2011

An Omaha four-piece making dance rock when the city's indie scene was loud. Two albums, Kill The Love and Get In Control, plus nights at Sokol Underground, The Waiting Room, and Slowdown. Spin.com noticed, and the band charted on CMJ without a label.

James, Albert, and David were already playing together when I met them at The Goofy Foot, where their band shared a bill with mine at the time, Ars Nova. We hit it off, I joined on bass, and together we started Go Motion. The band had never toured, so I booked our first couple of tours, and since I'd done some production work, I pushed for us to record at Smart Studios. I really believed in that band, and I still think we could have gone somewhere.

B3

Lazers Everywhere: Wal-mart

The Lazer Squad blasts onto the scene for the first time, in a Wal-mart.

It started over drinks at Brothers Lounge, when the guys came up with a hilarious idea: go places and shoot lazers out of our hands. I was just in the right place at the right time. The creativity was all Josh Foo and Travis Ahrenholtz, who knew how to shoot it, edit it, and add the visual effects.

B4

Eight Hours on Farnam

March 2020

One Saturday I ran up and down Farnam Street for eight hours straight, about 40 miles, to raise money for pancreatic cancer research with Project Purple. Friends and complete strangers took turns running alongside, and shops along the street sold purple pretzels.

What kept me going was a conversation from earlier that day with a man whose wife of 57 years had died of cancer.

B5

Anniversaire: Nightingale

2011

Anniversaire is an Omaha band led by Aaron Jordan, making lush, orchestral chamber pop. I engineered their album Nightingale at ARC (Another Recording Company) in Omaha, then joined them on percussion for the release show at The Waiting Room.

ARC is one of the best studios I've ever worked in, and I've made records at Black Lodge Recording, Smart Studios, and Tarquin Studios. When you have a perfect environment like that, the process is all about getting everyone on the same page and working toward a common goal. The tools, the people, and the songs are already there. If you have the desire, you can make something great.

An Anniversaire song, “Southerly,” later turned up in Patrick Wang's film The Grief of Others, which Aaron scored.
